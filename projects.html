Current Projects

Below, the main projects the lab is working on at the moment, alongside a global network of collaborators. Please feel free to get in touch if you are interested in any of these topics or have any questions, and see Join the lab if you would like to work on them with us!

Interactions between Neanderthals and Homo sapiens

We investigate Neanderthal population dynamics and how, where and when Neanderthals and Homo sapiens met and interacted, and what these encounters meant for both populations.

Lab member: Zélie Solan de Renty

Modelling genotype-phenotype dynamics

We develop spatially explicit models to understand how genetic variation translates into phenotypic diversity, and how demography, climate and selection shape this relationship across space and through time.

Climate-driven selection in baboons

In collaboration with Cosimo Posth, we study how past climate change has shaped natural selection in baboons, a primate found across a remarkably wide range of African environments.

Lab member: Amanda Solbirk Schott

Admixture mapping of African hunter-gatherer ancestry components

In collaboration with the Ioannidis Lab at Stanford University we use large-scale genomic datasets to explore the contributions of hunter-gatherer ancestry to complex traits and health outcomes.

Climate Informed Spatial Genetic Modelling (CISGeM)

Using paleoclimatic, genetic, archaeological and fossil data, we model how climate shaped human genetic and phenotypic diversity in different places and time periods.

Mobility and genetic diversity in Central Africa

We examine how patterns of mobility influence genetic diversity and population structure among Central African hunter-gatherers.

Integrating archaeological and genomic data to study cultural evolution

In collaboration with the Human Paleosystems Group at the Max Planck Institute of Geoanthropology, we integrate genomic and archaeological data to understand how changing population dynamics shape cultural evolution.

Mobility and settlement in the archaeological record

Alongside Prof. Matt Grove and Dr. James Blinkhorn from the University of Liverpool, we develop methods to recover signals of mobility, aggregation, and dispersal from lithic assemblages and site structures.

Settlement transitions in East Africa

Together with Dr Olympia Campbell from the Institute for Advanced Study in Toulouse, we investigate the causes and consequences of changes in mobility and spatial organization in Northern Kenya.

Evolutionary origins of central place foraging

Together with Dr Ketika Garg from Caltech, we use computational models to understand how central place foraging may have evolved in the genus Homo.

Past projects

Diversity in early learning experiences

Since 2019, Cecilia has worked in the Yucatán peninsula in collaboration with Dr Laura Shneidman on how changing socio-ecologies affect social and cultural practices. See Research for some of the publications from this project.

Hunter-gatherers' lifestyles in archaeology

In collaboration with Robert Bischoff and Dr Claudine Gravel-Miguel, Cecilia created a computational model to understand how the lifestyles of hunter-gatherers get reflected in archaeological assemblages (see Research).

Emergence of complex social networks from foraging behaviour

Alongside Dr Ketika Garg, Nicolás Restrepo Ochoa and Bleu Knight, Cecilia developed an agent-based model to understand the social networks emerging from hunter-gatherer foraging behaviour. See Research for our paper.

Genetic markers of cousin marriage and honour cultures

Cecilia collaborated with Olympia Campbell and Prof Ruth Mace to explore the relationship between cousin marriage, intensive kinship and honour cultures. See Research for our paper.

Spatio-temporally explicit models of mobility and cultural evolution

Alongside Dr Natasa Conrad and Johaness Zonker, Cecilia developed a model to assess how changing ecological landscapes shaped hunter-gatherer demography, mobility and cultural evolution. See Research for our paper.

Ecological drivers of ancient hunter-gatherers' lithic technologies

In collaboration with Dr James Blinkhorn and Prof Matt Grove, we worked on testing hypotheses about the drivers of cultural dynamics in the past.
